# Curio - Content, Sources, Rights and Editorial Strategy

## Central rule

Curio content is original explanatory content grounded in sources.

Books, papers and reports are research inputs.

They are not a text database to be copied into the product.

## Source hierarchy

Use the strongest appropriate source for each claim.

Preferred order:
1. primary peer-reviewed research
2. high-quality review or meta-analysis
3. major scientific institution or university
4. authoritative academic book
5. reputable science communication for orientation only

Popular books are excellent for:
- topic discovery
- conceptual framing
- identifying debates
- finding primary references

They should not be the only evidence for a contested scientific claim.

## Rights rules

For copyrighted commercial books:
- use only copies the user has lawful access to
- use them for research and reference
- do not publish copied passages
- do not create chapter-by-chapter derivative reproductions
- do not synthesize TTS from copyrighted source text
- verify quotation rights before any quotation beyond minimal fair reference

For public-domain and open-access sources:
- record the exact license
- respect attribution, noncommercial and no-derivatives clauses
- do not assume open access means unrestricted commercial reuse

For sources with unclear provenance or rights:
- mark rights_status = unverified
- do not use their text directly in publishable content
- replace with lawful editions or primary/open sources when possible

## Curio editorial workflow

1. Define a learning question or concept.
2. Gather research sources.
3. Build a source map.
4. Separate established knowledge from hypotheses and active debate.
5. Draft original Curio explanation.
6. Map each important claim to source IDs.
7. Create rubric and misconceptions.
8. Scientific/editorial review.
9. Approve text version.
10. Generate audio from the approved Curio text.
11. Publish.
12. Re-review when sources or text change materially.

## Claim-source mapping

Every important scientific statement should be traceable.

Recommended relation fields:
- content_type
- content_id
- source_id
- claim_or_section
- source_role
- confidence
- notes

Source roles:
- primary evidence
- review
- background
- historical
- debate/opposing view
- inspiration only

## Scientific nuance

Content should explicitly distinguish:
- well-established result
- useful simplification
- plausible but uncertain mechanism
- active scientific debate
- historical claim that has been revised

Curio should never flatten scientific disagreement into false certainty.

## AI-generated drafts

AI may help:
- summarize research internally
- suggest concept connections
- draft original explanations
- propose rubric points

AI may not:
- invent citations
- publish its draft automatically
- turn one book author's interpretation into settled fact
- bypass editorial review

## Audio rights

The source of truth for TTS is Curio's approved original text.

Audio assets are versioned against that text.

If text changes materially, the old audio is stale.
